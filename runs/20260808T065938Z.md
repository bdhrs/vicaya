# Run Reflection: khuddaka-late-suttas-metta-ratana-mangala

## What was researched

Arguments supporting the scholarly view that the Karaniyamettā Sutta (Khp 9 / Snp 1.8), Ratana Sutta (Khp 6 / Snp 2.1), and Maṅgala Sutta (Khp 5 / Snp 2.4) are later additions to the Pāḷi canon — composed or compiled centuries after the Buddha.

## Key findings

The strongest finding is the self-referential commentary admission: Paramatthajotikā I (KHPa1 para 1) explicitly states the Khuddakapāṭha "was arranged by the lineage of teachers as a pathway for recitation, not as spoken by the Blessed One" (*na bhagavatā abhāsitaṃ*). This is the clearest available evidence and requires no inferential leap.

Secondary arguments — Khuddakapāṭha as the latest KN stratum (von Hinüber, Warder), Suttanipāta stratigraphy placing chapters 1–2 as later than 4–5 (Norman), absence of Āgama parallels, and the institutionalized paritta development history — all converge on late compilation.

The Ratana Sutta has a partial Sanskrit parallel in the Mahāvastu (*Svastyanāgātha*, san-lo-mvu29), which complicates a pure "late Theravāda invention" reading for that sutta specifically.

The compilation vs. composition distinction is essential: evidence for late Khuddakapāṭha compilation is strong; evidence for late composition of the individual verses is weaker and more inferential.

## Key errors and corrections

1. **Fabricated verse at SNP13 para 226.** The draft initially quoted "Yāvatā devamanussā āsajjanti sukhesino..." as the Ratana Sutta truth-act formula, but direct SQL confirmed this verse does not exist in `s0501m_mul` or `s0505m_mul`. The actual para 226 text ("Yaṃ kiñci vittaṃ idha vā huraṃ vā...") was substituted.

2. **Karaniyamettā tier slip.** The draft characterized the sutta as arriving "pre-theorized as a protective device, not as a teaching on mettā." This was incorrect about the verse text itself — the sutta IS a meditation instruction. The confusion was between the commentary nidāna (protective framing) and the sutta text. Corrected to separate these layers explicitly.

3. **PJ I vs. PJ II.** The developed forest-monks nidāna was attributed to PJ I; SQL search of s0501a_att vs. s0505a_att confirmed the rukkhadevā narrative is in s0505a_att (PJ II). PJ I's treatment is brief.

## What was blocked or missed

- Library volume not mounted: Norman (#10836) and von Hinüber (#6246) not directly read. Both core arguments rest on Wikipedia and Encyclopedia of Buddhism summaries. This is the single largest methodological weakness in the note.
- t211.39 (Taisho) Maṅgala parallel: content unknown. If a real parallel exists, the "no external parallels" argument for Maṅgala requires revision.
- Anālayo's published EBT comparative work on parallel absence not directly engaged; only his position paraphrased from secondary context.
