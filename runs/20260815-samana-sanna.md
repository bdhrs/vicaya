## Summary of findings

The three samaṇasaññā (AN10.101) are: (1) *vevaṇṇiyamhi ajjhupagato* — "I have become casteless"; (2) *parapaṭibaddhā me jīvikā* — "My livelihood depends on others"; (3) *añño me ākappo karaṇīyo* — "My deportment must be different." When cultivated (*bhāvitā bahulīkatā*), they fulfill seven qualities: continuous virtue, non-covetousness, non-malice, non-conceit, desire for training, purposive use of requisites, and aroused energy.

The same three appear as the first three of the ten daily reflections of AN10.48, there framed as *paccavekkhaṇā* (frequent recollection) rather than formal cultivation objects. The two framings are complementary: daily reflection keeps the ground fertile; meditative cultivation builds specific ethical qualities.

**Critical translation finding:** The CST-generated English renders *vevaṇṇiyamhi* as "wearing patched-up robes" — an error. DPD (vevaṇṇiya 1) is unambiguous: "state of having no caste." Thanissaro's "I have become casteless" is correct.

**Historical context:** The śramaṇa category is attested in Sanskrit sources including Kautilya's *Arthaśāstra* (institutional social category), Āryaśūra's *Jātakamālā*, and Asaṅga's *Abhidharmasamuccayabhāṣya*. Buddhist samaṇadhamma redefines the śramaṇa identity away from external austerities toward inner cultivation (cf. MN 39–40).

**Modern teachers:** No sustained modern commentary on samaṇasaññā as a standalone bhāvanā was found. Thanissaro emphasizes "casteless" and describes the monastery as an "economy of gifts" that structurally enacts the second perception. Bodhi (via existing note) frames dependence as reciprocal spiritual partnership.

**Āgama parallels:** None identified for either AN10.101 or AN10.48 (EBC confirmed).
